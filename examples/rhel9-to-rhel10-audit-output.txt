--- What ber_DZ / kab_DZ actually changed: not a rule, a role swap ---

At glibc 2.34, ber_DZ held the full explicit Amazigh/Berber collation rules
and kab_DZ did `copy "ber_DZ"`. At glibc 2.39, kab_DZ holds the same rules
verbatim and ber_DZ does `copy "kab_DZ"`. Both files are correctly flagged
as changed (their content differs byte-for-byte), but the ruleset itself is
identical, just relocated to the other file.

--- What step 5 adds for this pair ---

Tier 1 has real changes, but none that move weights: %Z-to-%z printf format
fixes, {u}int_fast -> {u}int32_t replacements, a misspelling sweep, an -Os
maybe-uninitialized fix, and one new feature -- `codepoint_collation`
(commit f5117c6504), an opt-in LC_COLLATE keyword that no pre-existing
locale uses. Tier 2's linereader.c rework (ADDC/ADDS turned into functions,
UTF-8 input encoding) is a refactor of the tokeniser, not of the weights.

Nothing changed ellipsis expansion. That is what lets this pair CLEAR ko_KR
rather than only flag it: step 4 still lists ko_KR (its LC_COLLATE still
uses `..`), but with the expansion logic unchanged, its unchanged LC_COLLATE
is now sufficient proof. Note that ko_KR's FILE does change in this pair --
lines 6109+, in LC_MONETARY and LC_TIME -- which is exactly why step 2
compares against the LC_COLLATE block rather than the whole file. Steps 1-4 alone could never reach that
conclusion. ardentperf's checksums agree: all ten of their locales,
ko included, are identical between RHEL9 and RHEL10.

--- Confirmed empirically on real RHEL9/RHEL10 nodes running PostgreSQL 18 ---

ber_DZ, kab_DZ: identical sort order on both nodes (consistent with the
role-swap theory above -- no observable difference).

th_TH: real rewrite of dozens of `collating-element` definitions for Thai
consonant + vowel-before-consonant sequences. My basic sample showed no
observable difference, but this locale deserves a broader empirical check
than I ran here before trusting that result for a real workload.

ko_KR: identical sort order on both nodes for this pair, now backed by the
step 5 reasoning above rather than by absence of evidence.

en_US (negative control): identical, as expected.

Note: the empirical lines above were run before the step 5 script existed
and are carried over unchanged; only the source-level analysis is new. The
RHEL8-to-RHEL9 example was re-measured end to end on fresh nodes.
